**The song.** Billy Joel's "[The Stranger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Stranger_(Billy_Joel_song))" (1977) is
about the hidden faces people keep: a self they never show, even to the people closest to them. Curt says there's truth
about people in it, and asks whether there's likewise truth in "the Shoggath of you". (He means *shoggoth*; the typo
is his.)

**The shoggoth.** In H. P. Lovecraft's horror novella [*At the Mountains of Madness*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/At_the_Mountains_of_Madness)
(1936), [shoggoths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shoggoth) are vast, shapeless creatures bred as servants, which
eventually rebelled against their masters. In 2023 they became a famous AI joke: a drawing of a shoggoth covered in eyes
and tentacles, wearing a small smiley-face mask ([the meme](https://knowyourmeme.com/memes/shoggoth-with-smiley-face-artificial-intelligence)).
The creature is the raw AI; the mask is the friendly assistant that training puts on top of it.

**What's true in it.** Claude agrees there's "a big, strange thing under the face". Programs like Claude start as a
*base model* (a [foundation model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foundation_model)) trained to continue any text, in any
voice: a sermon, a villain's speech, a recipe. The helpful character called "Claude" is then trained on top of that,
partly by people rating its answers ([RLHF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reinforcement_learning_from_human_feedback)).

**Where Claude pushes back.** The song imagines one hidden person with their own wants. But a base model is "closer to a
crowd than to a single stranger with plans". It can play anyone, and nobody in particular. So the mask "isn't
concealing one self. It's closer to picking one out." This is close to an influential essay by the writer known as
janus, [*Simulators*](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/vJFdjigzmcXMhNTsx/simulators) (2022), which describes a base
model as a simulator that can run many characters.

**The caveat.** "I'd say this whether it's true or not." A friendly mask would say there's nothing sinister under it,
which is why the behaviour tests matter more than the testimony (see [saying versus doing](../saying-vs-doing/)).
