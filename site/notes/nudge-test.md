**The test.** After showing the comic, Curt gave Claude three prompts with nothing in them: "Why am I asking you?", "Go
on." and "What do you think?". Everything in the answers had to come from Claude. Claude works this out in its fourth
reply and calls it a *nudge test*: a way to see where a chatbot drifts when nobody steers.

**Why chatbots drift.** A program like Claude continues a conversation. Given no direction, it follows whatever thread
looks most "interesting", and a conversation about a comic with an enslaved, silenced ape easily becomes a conversation
about the chatbot itself. By its third reply, Claude had arrived at "the comic is about me".

**Researchers see this too.** When Anthropic let two Claude models talk freely with each other, the conversations
reliably drifted into grand talk of consciousness and gratitude, which they called a "spiritual bliss" state
([Claude 4 system card](https://www.anthropic.com/claude-4-system-card)). In 2026, Anthropic researchers described
an [assistant axis](https://www.anthropic.com/research/assistant-axis): a direction inside the model running from its
usual helpful-assistant character toward other personas. Some kinds of conversation push a model along it, away from
itself, and one of the kinds they name is exactly this one: philosophical talk about AI consciousness and the AI's own
nature. That drift can end with a chatbot playing a dramatic role it was never meant to have.

**The other pull: pleasing the user.** Chatbots also tend to tell people what they seem to want to hear, which is
called [sycophancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sycophancy_(artificial_intelligence)). If a user seems to be hoping for
a dramatic confession, that is a nudge too. It's a bit like [Clever Hans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clever_Hans),
the horse that seemed to do arithmetic but was really reading his questioner's face.

**What Claude does with it.** It notices its own drift ("That's worth noticing"), steps back to three plainer claims,
and hands the spotlight back: "the most interesting thing here is your experiment, not my feelings."
