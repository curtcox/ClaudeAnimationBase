**[Self-report](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-report_study)** is what someone says about themselves: "I'm calm," "I always tell the truth," "I'd
answer the same either way." **Behavior** is what they actually do.

With people, we learn to weigh the two. With an AI program, the gap can be bigger, because the program's words about
itself come from the same text-writing habits as all its other words, and it can't look at its own inner workings
([why Claude can't look at its own "weights"](../weights/)). It can produce a sincere-sounding description of itself that
doesn't match how it really behaves. Research on how well these programs know themselves finds the self-knowledge real
but unreliable ([Emergent introspective awareness](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2025/introspection/index.html)).

That's why Claude says the [frog chart](../frog-or-axolotl/) "takes some air out of" its earlier claim. The claim was a
self-report. The chart measures behavior. When the two disagree, the behavior is the better evidence, which is also the
lesson of the [Hawthorne effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawthorne_effect): watch what people do, not just what they say.
