**The puzzle.** Ned Block's "Blockhead" (see [the case against AI concepts](../concepts-case/)) is a machine that looks up
every reply in a gigantic table. It would pass any conversation test, yet it plainly doesn't think. So, the argument goes,
passing tests proves nothing about a mind.

**Where Curt points.** [Eliezer Yudkowsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eliezer_Yudkowsky), a writer and researcher
best known for warning about AI risk, discusses this in his collection of essays [*Rationality: From AI to Zombies*](https://www.readthesequences.com/).
The essay is called "[GAZP vs. GLUT](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/k6EPphHiBH4WWYFCj/gazp-vs-glut)" (2008).
- **GLUT** is his name for the Giant Look-Up Table.
- **GAZP** is his "Generalized Anti-Zombie Principle". A [philosophical zombie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophical_zombie)
  is an imagined being that behaves exactly like a person, talking, laughing and complaining about pain, while
  experiencing nothing at all ([more](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/zombies/)). Yudkowsky argues that the things
  which make us *say* we're conscious are the same things that make us conscious, so a perfect zombie isn't really
  possible.

**The argument, in one step.** A table that gives intelligent answers to every possible conversation is astronomically
large, far bigger than the universe. It can't be filled in by chance. Something had to work out every one of those
answers, and "whatever did the filling did the thinking". So Blockhead doesn't show that intelligence can be faked
without thought. It just moves the thinking to whoever built the table.

**Applied to Claude.** Claude isn't a lookup table, but the question of where its abilities came from has the same
answer: its training, on "a vast amount of human thought". Whatever Claude has or lacks, the thinking behind its answers
started with people.
