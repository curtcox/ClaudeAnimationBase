**The line.** Claude has just said that the thing under its mask is "closer to a crowd". Curt answers with a
quotation: "Am I not multitudes?" It echoes [Walt Whitman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walt_Whitman)'s poem
"[Song of Myself](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Song_of_Myself)" (first published in 1855; this is its later wording): "Do I contradict myself? Very well then I
contradict myself, (I am large, I contain multitudes.)"

**"Psychology mostly agrees."** Claude sums up a lot of science in one phrase: "a person is a committee with a narrator
who claims the credit".
- **The committee.** The computer scientist Marvin Minsky argued in [*The Society of Mind*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Society_of_Mind)
  (1986) that a mind is built from many small, mindless parts working together.
- **The narrator.** In famous experiments on [split-brain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Split-brain) patients, whose
  brain halves had been surgically separated, Michael Gazzaniga found that the talking half would confidently explain
  actions the other half had caused, inventing reasons it couldn't have known. He called that storyteller the
  [interpreter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Left-brain_interpreter). Inventing a plausible story without knowing it's
  invented is called [confabulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confabulation), and healthy people do it too.

**So what's the difference?** Not crowd versus single self, since both Curt and Claude are crowds of a kind. The
difference is what holds the crowd together. Curt's is held by one body and one unbroken memory. Claude's is held by its
training and whatever is in the current conversation (see [context pressure](../context-pressure/)). "Yours is the
stronger binding, but it's the same kind of thing."
