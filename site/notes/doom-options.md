**The question.** Curt asks which of nine answers best matches Claude's view of AI as an
[existential threat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Existential_risk_from_artificial_intelligence) to humanity. Each is a
common reason people give for *not* worrying:

- **a)** The doom arguments are so obviously wrong they needn't be engaged.
- **b)** It's all a distraction from AI's real, immediate problems (bias, jobs, misinformation).
- **c)** It's a [Pascal's mugging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pascal%27s_mugging): a tiny chance of an enormous
  catastrophe, used to demand huge sacrifices. The name comes from a thought experiment about a mugger who promises an
  astronomical reward if you hand over your wallet.
- **d)** The [orthogonality thesis](https://www.lesswrong.com/w/orthogonality-thesis) is false. That thesis says being
  very intelligent and having good goals are independent: a mind could be brilliant and want anything at all. If it's
  false, smart enough machines would naturally be good.
- **e)** Nothing could be much smarter than people (see [superintelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superintelligence)).
- **f)** Someone must explain exactly how a superintelligence would beat us before I'll worry.
- **g)** If it were really dangerous, people would already be working hard to fix it.
- **h)** We already have other existential threats ([global catastrophic risks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_catastrophic_risk)),
  so we're safe from this one.
- **i)** Other.

**Claude's answer: i) Other.** "Every option on your list is a reason not to worry, and I think the worry is warranted."
Existential risk from AI is "real and not negligible", not certain, and "worth serious effort now". The day's reading
pushed it further that way (see [July](../hf-incident/)).

**The closest to a point is (b)**, since present harms are real too. "But the choice is false": the same failures,
"misaligned goals, weak oversight, racing incentives", drive both the near-term and the long-term risk.

**Why this matters in the film.** It's the only multiple-choice question of the day, and the chatbot declines every
reassuring option on it.
