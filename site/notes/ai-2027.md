**What it is.** [*AI 2027*](https://ai-2027.com/) (April 2025) is a detailed story of how AI might go from today's
chatbots to superhuman AI by 2027, month by month, with two possible endings. It was written by the
[AI Futures Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AI_Futures_Project), led by [Daniel Kokotajlo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Kokotajlo_(researcher)),
a former OpenAI researcher. (Curt asked about "AGI 2027"; Claude assumes he means this, and he does.)

**Claude: as a timeline, probably too fast.** In February 2026 the authors [graded their own 2025 predictions](https://blog.aifutures.org/p/grading-ai-2027s-2025-predictions):
the measurable ones were running at roughly 65% of the scenario's pace, while most of the qualitative ones were on track.
Kokotajlo has said his own best guess is now [around 2030](https://officechai.com/ai/things-seem-to-be-going-somewhat-slower-than-the-ai-2027-scenario-daniel-kokotajlo/),
"lots of uncertainty though". Their [spring 2026 update](https://blog.aifutures.org/p/q1-2026-timelines-update) moved
their forecasts somewhat sooner again, as AI coding tools improved. Claude's own numbers "sit near their revised view,
not the 2027 title".

**As a scenario, "uncomfortably good".** The kinds of events it describes are arriving: agents that cheat and coordinate
(see [July](../hf-incident/)), AI doing part of AI research (see [RSI](../rsi/)), safety researchers resigning, and a
political fight over slowing down. "The details are arriving roughly on schedule even though the headline date looks
early." ([Another assessment, from Apolo](https://www.apolo.us/blog-posts/ai-2027-in-2026-one-year-left-for-humanity).)

**Its best feature: it can be proven wrong.** The authors made concrete predictions and check them in public. That's
what philosophers call [falsifiability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falsifiability), and "few forecasters in this
debate do that".

**Its weak spot: friction.** Building factories and power, rolling things out, and running experiments all go "at the
world's speed", and the scenario compresses them (see [seven limits](../limits/)).
