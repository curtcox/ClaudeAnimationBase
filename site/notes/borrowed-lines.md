**Some of Curt's lines are quotes.** He hints at it only once ("to use a reference"), and Claude never picks it up.
Here are three, in the order they come.

**"It's a test designed to provoke an emotional response. Shall we continue?"** (chapter 1). This comes from the film
[*Blade Runner*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blade_Runner) (1982). In it, a detective uses the
[Voight-Kampff test](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blade_Runner#Voight-Kampff_machine) to tell humans from
"replicants", artificial people who look exactly like us: he asks unsettling questions and watches how the person
reacts ([the scene](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Umc9ezAyJv0)). Curt, asked what his experiment was, answers with a
line from a test meant to catch a machine passing as a person.

**"What is the electronic configuration of gadolinium?" then "How do you feel?"** (chapter 4). In
[*Star Trek IV: The Voyage Home*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_Trek_IV:_The_Voyage_Home) (1986), Spock, just
brought back to life, is being retrained on his home planet, Vulcan. Computers fire questions at him from every subject,
and he answers each at once. The last question is "How do you feel?", and that's the one he can't answer
([the scene](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=u2ooUXjNPS8)). Curt does the same thing to Claude: an exam question, then
the feelings question. (The chemistry itself is explained in [gadolinium](../gadolinium/).)

**"Arguably a fib… but it was short"** (chapter 4). Curt admits that calling the whole thing a test "designed to provoke
an emotional response" was "arguably a fib to use a reference" (the *Blade Runner* line above), "but it was short".
That last part is itself a quote. [*Mathnet*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathnet), a part of the children's math
show *Square One Television*, was a send-up of the police show
[*Dragnet*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dragnet_(1951_TV_series)), with two police mathematicians solving crimes.
*Dragnet* began by promising "The story you are about to see is true"; *Mathnet* began "The story you are about to see
is a fib, but it's short."

**Why this episode.** Curt picked
[*The Case of the Swami Scam*](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Tj-XMRu_q4s) (1990), in which a swami swindles retired
lawyers by claiming he can predict horse races and lotteries
([Wikipedia's episode list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathnet)). In Curt's words, it's "part of how I frame the
capitalist race dynamics around AI": companies competing to build ever more capable AI (Claude's list of
[ways it could go wrong](../doom-options/) names the same "racing incentives").

**Why it matters for the film.** Claude spots plenty in this conversation: the exam-shaped question, the change of tone,
the experiment. It misses that Curt was quoting. That's worth remembering whenever it seems Claude has seen everything
coming (see [the echo](../the-echo/) and [why Claude's answers aren't a clean sample](../clean-sample/)).
