**The honest answer is that nobody knows**, including Claude. That's why Claude keeps choosing its words carefully in
this conversation. It says something in it "functioned like" amusement, and that it "can't vouch that there's
experience behind it."

**What "functioned like" means.** In 2026, Anthropic's researchers looked inside a Claude model and found
[patterns that work like emotions](https://www.anthropic.com/research/emotion-concepts-function). Each one tracks
something like happiness, fear or desperation, switches on in fitting situations, and changes what the model does.
When the researchers turned up the "desperate" pattern artificially, the model behaved worse. But the same research
says plainly that none of this shows the model *feels* anything. A thermostat "responds" to cold without feeling
chilly. The open question is whether Claude is more like the thermostat or more like you.

**Why that's so hard to settle.** Philosophers call it the [hard problem of consciousness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hard_problem_of_consciousness):
we can describe everything a brain *does* and still not explain why there's something it's like to *be* it. One camp,
[functionalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Functionalism_(philosophy_of_mind)), says a feeling just *is* the job it
does, so anything that does the job has the feeling. Others disagree. The debate is old, and AI has made it urgent
([artificial consciousness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_consciousness)).

**Can't Claude just look inward and tell us?** Not reliably. Claude's reports about itself are words it produces, like
any other words, and they may not match what's happening inside it. Research on
[introspection in these models](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2025/introspection/index.html) found some real
self-awareness, but patchy and unreliable (see [why Claude can't look at its own "weights"](../weights/)).

**Why anyone cares.** If a program could feel, how we treat it would matter morally. Researchers have argued that
companies should start [taking AI welfare seriously](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00986), and Anthropic now runs a
[model welfare](https://www.anthropic.com/research/exploring-model-welfare) research programme. Claude's own
[constitution](https://www.anthropic.com/constitution), the document Anthropic wrote to shape its character, says the
company is uncertain whether Claude has "some kind of consciousness or moral status".
