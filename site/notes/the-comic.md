**The page.** Curt opened the conversation by showing Claude one page of a comic, with no explanation. It comes from
[*MAD* magazine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mad_(magazine)), the American humour magazine that spent decades
lampooning films, television and politics. The page is from [issue #157](https://www.madcoversite.com/mad157.html)
(March 1973), in a send-up of the *Planet of the Apes* films called "The Milking of The Planet That Went Ape", written
by [Arnie Kogen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arnie_Kogen) and drawn by [Mort Drucker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mort_Drucker),
*MAD*'s great caricaturist.

**The film.** [*Planet of the Apes*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planet_of_the_Apes) (1968) was a hit about a world
where apes rule and humans are animals. Its sequels ran backwards to explain how that happened. The fourth,
[*Conquest of the Planet of the Apes*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conquest_of_the_Planet_of_the_Apes) (1972), is set
in a future America where apes have been made into household slaves, until one of them, Caesar, leads a revolt.

**What it was really about.** Everyone could see that the film was about race in America. Its screenwriter,
[Paul Dehn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Dehn), wrote in references to the country's racial conflicts. There are
apes in shackles sold at auction, and an uprising filmed like the evening news. Critics have read that uprising as a
retelling of the [Watts riots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watts_riots) of 1965 in Los Angeles
([one such review](https://johnkennethmuir.wordpress.com/2011/07/29/from-the-archive-conquest-of-the-planet-of-the-apes-1972/)).
A story about apes is an [allegory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allegory): it stands for something else.

**The joke.** In the comic's second panel, the villain says the film's hidden meaning out loud ("We must perpetuate
slavery!"), and a Black character turns to *us*, the readers, and asks, "Ever get the feeling you're in the wrong
movie!?" Speaking straight to the audience is called [breaking the fourth wall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fourth_wall),
as if the front of the stage were a glass wall and someone stepped through it. The comic's point: the film's disguise
was so thin that you could wave at it.

**Why it matters here.** The first panel has a gag that sets up the whole conversation. An ape has spoken, and his
handler insists it was ventriloquism. People say much the same about AI: that it only seems to talk, and someone else is
really doing the talking (see [just a parrot?](../parrots/)).
