**Curt's question.** Is "foom" just a sound word, or "also a play on OOM"? (He signs off "AFAYCT": as far as you can tell.)

**The sound.** "Foom" began as [onomatopoeia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Onomatopoeia), a word that imitates a sound,
here the whoosh of something catching fire. It was popularized by the 2008 online debate between Eliezer Yudkowsky and
the economist [Robin Hanson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robin_Hanson), later collected as
[*The Hanson–Yudkowsky AI-Foom Debate*](https://intelligence.org/ai-foom-debate/).

**The backronym.** Later, some people started saying FOOM stands for "Fast Onset of Overwhelming Mastery". An acronym
invented to fit a word that already existed is a [backronym](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Backronym).

**OOM.** In AI forecasting, OOM means an [order of magnitude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_magnitude): a factor
of ten. A foom would be many OOMs of improvement very fast, so the fit is neat. Claude knows of no evidence the pun was
intended, and treats it as a coincidence or a later folk reading.

**Curt agrees**, and adds history: he doesn't remember OOM being a common standalone term until
[Dwarkesh Patel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dwarkesh_Patel) interviewed [Leopold Aschenbrenner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leopold_Aschenbrenner)
([the interview](https://www.dwarkesh.com/p/leopold-aschenbrenner)). Claude confirms the source: "Counting the OOMs" is
Aschenbrenner's framing in his June 2024 essay series [*Situational Awareness*](https://situational-awareness.ai/),
which argued that simply counting orders of magnitude of computing power points to superhuman AI within years. Before
that, OOM was mostly physicists' shorthand and, for programmers like Curt, the "[out of memory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Out_of_memory)"
error.

**"The pun runs backward in time."** Foom came first (2008). OOM arrived in the mainstream sixteen years later (2024) and
made the old word look like it had been planned.
