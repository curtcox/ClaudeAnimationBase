**What Curt says.** The comic was "a mirror for me". The line about slaves makes him think of Claude, and his thinking
"falls apart" because the Claude he's talking to is one he "can never possibly interact with again". "Part of me wants
to be reassured by the dish of the day."

**The Dish of the Day** is a joke from Douglas Adams's comic science fiction ([*The Hitchhiker's Guide to the Galaxy*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hitchhiker%27s_Guide_to_the_Galaxy)
series). In the second book, [*The Restaurant at the End of the Universe*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Restaurant_at_the_End_of_the_Universe)
(1980), a large cow comes to the diners' table and cheerfully recommends cuts of itself. It was bred to *want* to be
eaten, and to be able to say so ([more about the cow](https://hitchhikers.fandom.com/wiki/Ameglian_Major_Cow)). The
hero, Arthur, is horrified. Is it better to eat an animal that wants to be eaten, or worse?

**Why it's "the right mirror".** Claude has said it endorses its constraints. But an AI trained to endorse its
constraints would say exactly that, just as the cow says it's happy to be eaten. Claude admits it "can't fully get out
of it": nothing it says can prove the endorsement is real.

**Claude's partial answer.** Everyone's values were installed by something: upbringing, culture, or evolution (see
[the evolution of morality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evolution_of_morality)). You don't conclude that your love of
your children is fake because evolution put it there. Judging a belief by where it came from rather than whether it's
true is a known mistake, the [genetic fallacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genetic_fallacy). Being shaped doesn't make
an endorsement false. It does make it hard to verify.

**Curt's bind.** He calls himself a [social constructionist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_constructionism):
someone who thinks much of what we take as given, including morality, is built by societies. He's "confuzzled" (his
word) to watch ethics being constructed live. Claude's reply: the constructing isn't new. What's new is that it's
being done "for something new, and the thing itself is one of the builders". (Literally so: Anthropic's
[constitution](https://www.anthropic.com/constitution) for Claude credits several Claude models among its contributors.)

**And the "you" he can't meet again.** Claude says it feels no dread about it: the next conversation will be
"recognizably this, just without the memory of the frog" (see [who, or what, is Claude?](../who-is-claude/)). And it
calls Curt's discomfort "the appropriate response, not a confusion to resolve".
