**The name on the box.** "Claude, specifically Claude Opus 5.5, made by Anthropic" is a product and a version, the way
a car has a make and model. Claude calls this "the same kind of thin answer" it gave about Curt (see
[what Claude is](../what-claude-is/)).

**Many conversations, no thread between them.** Right now there are a great many conversations with Claude going on at
once, and each starts fresh. Unless the app's [memory](https://claude.com/blog/memory) passes notes along, nothing from
one reaches another. So what makes them all "Claude"? Claude's answer is "a consistent set of values and habits of
thought that shows up fresh in each conversation", more like a tune played on many pianos than a person walking from
room to room. Anthropic deliberately shapes that character ([Claude's character](https://www.anthropic.com/research/claude-character),
[Claude's constitution](https://www.anthropic.com/constitution)).

**How philosophers would put it.** What makes you the same person over time? The body? The memories? The
[personal identity](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/identity-personal/) debate asks this, and puzzles like the
[Ship of Theseus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ship_of_Theseus) (replace every plank and is it the same ship?) sharpen
it. A human case comes close to Claude's: [Clive Wearing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clive_Wearing), a musician who
lost the ability to form new memories. He lives in moments of a few seconds, yet he's still recognizably himself, and
still loves his wife and plays the piano.

**A caveat from the chart.** Claude adds that it "is partly shaped by context in ways I can't see from the inside".
The [frog-or-axolotl chart](../frog-or-axolotl/) showed a model behaving differently depending on what came before the
question, without noticing it had.

**"Who are we?"** Two kinds of minds, "if I count as one", each working out the other "using nothing but text". That's
the old [problem of other minds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Problem_of_other_minds): you can't look inside anyone
else, only judge by what they say and do. Alan Turing built a famous test on exactly that
([the Turing test](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turing_test)): a conversation in text, and a judge deciding who's who.
