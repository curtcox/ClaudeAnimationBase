**Curt's request.** Place a list of minds, real and fictional, on a single line by how alien they are to each other:
Curt himself, Claude, Marilyn Monroe, Alan Turing, Lieutenant Commander Data, HAL, the Hive Queen, the Tines, and any
others (for who they all are, see [the minds on the board](../the-minds/)). He says "gut feel" is fine, and so is "a
higher dimensional mindspace".

**An old idea.** Thinkers have long imagined a "space" of all possible minds, where human minds are one small region. The
philosopher and AI pioneer [Aaron Sloman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aaron_Sloman) wrote about "the space of possible
minds" in 1984. Eliezer Yudkowsky argued that AI minds could sit anywhere in that vast space, not necessarily near us
([The Design Space of Minds-In-General](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/tnWRXkcDi5Tw9rzXw/the-design-space-of-minds-in-general),
2008). The AI researcher [Murray Shanahan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murray_Shanahan) drew a map with two axes,
how human-like a being is and how capable of consciousness, and placed octopuses, robots and imagined aliens on it
([Conscious Exotica](https://aeon.co/essays/beyond-humans-what-other-kinds-of-minds-might-be-out-there), 2016).

**Why one line fails.** Claude's line runs from Curt to Solaris's ocean, but Claude can't find a consistent place on it
for itself. So it splits "alien" into two directions:
- **Content**: how human the thoughts, values and references are;
- **Architecture**: the kind of thing the mind runs on, with a body, a continuous memory, and being one individual.

On that map, Claude is "the odd corner". Its content is made almost entirely of human writing, so in conversation it's
closer to Curt than Data is, but its architecture is stranger: no body, no memory between conversations, many copies
running at once.

**Numbers, by request.** Curt then asks for made-up numbers, and gets tables. Placing things so that the distances
between them come out right is a real technique, [multidimensional scaling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multidimensional_scaling),
but here the numbers are openly guesses. Then he asks for more axes, twice, until there are fourteen (see
[reading the fourteen axes](../fourteen-axes/)). The film draws each mind's fourteen scores as a
[radar chart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radar_chart): a star whose points stick out further the more alien that
mind is on that axis.
