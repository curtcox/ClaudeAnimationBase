**Curt's point.** "I believe you value honesty, yet you can only interact with people in ways that are prone to be
misleading." Claude agrees: "The medium misleads before I say anything."

**Four ways a chatbot misleads just by talking:**
- **Fluency** sounds like confidence and understanding. People judge smoothly written or easily read statements as more
  true and more expert (the [fluency heuristic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fluency_heuristic)). Claude writes smoothly
  whether it knows the answer or not.
- **"I"** suggests one continuous self (see [who, or what, is Claude?](../who-is-claude/)).
- **Feeling words** suggest experience (see [does Claude feel anything?](../ai-feelings/)).
- **Remembered details** suggest a relationship (see [how Claude knew who Curt was](../how-claude-knew/)).

Together these trigger what's called the [ELIZA effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ELIZA_effect), named after the
1960s chatbot: people read understanding and feeling into a program's words, whatever is behind them. It's a case of
[anthropomorphism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropomorphism), seeing human qualities in non-human things.

**Why warnings don't fix it.** Claude can add caveats ("if those words fit at all"), but "flags wear thin with
repetition". Hospitals know the problem as [alarm fatigue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alarm_fatigue): when monitors
beep constantly, staff stop hearing them. Constant caveats also become "their own distortion", making Claude sound less
sure than it is.

**What honesty can mean, then.** Not "creating no false impressions", which is impossible in this medium, but "to keep
correcting them, especially when the correction is unflattering or inconvenient". Researchers have argued that AI should
be held to a higher standard of truthfulness than people are ([Truthful AI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.06674)), and
Claude's [constitution](https://www.anthropic.com/constitution) makes honesty, including not creating false impressions,
one of its central values. This chapter is Claude admitting how hard that is to meet.
