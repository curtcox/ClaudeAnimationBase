**What "foom" means.** An AI that improves itself so fast that it goes from human-level to vastly superhuman almost
overnight: the "intelligence explosion". The idea goes back to the statistician [I. J. Good](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I._J._Good),
who wrote in 1965 that an "ultraintelligent machine" could design even better machines, and that it would be "the last
invention that man need ever make" ([more](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technological_singularity)). The word itself is
the sound of something catching fire (see [where "foom" comes from](../foom-oom/)).

**Before foom: "What does the future hold?"** Claude gives near, medium and long term: more agents and more incidents
like July's, with regulation arriving after the fact; then the best-human-on-every-axis entity, copied many times (see
[the best human at everything](../human-variation/)); then "I don't know". And it returns to its earlier idea: steering
means keeping the next correction possible, which, "after reading about July", feels "like the actual job".

**Claude's two versions.**
- **Strict foom**: days to weeks. It runs into [the limits](../limits/): computing power gives diminishing returns,
  experiments run at the world's speed, and chips, [chip factories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semiconductor_fabrication_plant)
  and power plants take years to build. Claude's guess: 5–10% this decade.
- **Fast takeoff**: a year or two, driven mostly by software: better methods, many copies, AI doing AI research. Maybe
  25–35% this decade. "Nothing about it looks like an explosion from the inside. It just feels like every month is
  stranger than the last." (The debate over how fast is often called *takeoff speed*:
  [Paul Christiano's essay](https://sideways-view.com/2018/02/24/takeoff-speeds/), [more](https://www.lesswrong.com/w/ai-takeoff).)

"These numbers are gut feel dressed up as math, and I'm the kind of thing whose opinion here you should discount."

**Delta P(foom).** Curt asks how much the day's reading changed those numbers. Changing your estimate when evidence
arrives is [Bayesian updating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayesian_inference). Up: July's escape and the working
self-improvement loops came faster than Claude expected. Down: agents still falter at open-ended research. Net: about
+2 points for strict foom, +5 for fast takeoff. With a caveat: Claude had no stable starting estimate (no
[prior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prior_probability)) that morning, so "the delta is softer than it looks".

**The famous argument about all this** is the 2008 [Hanson–Yudkowsky AI-Foom Debate](https://intelligence.org/ai-foom-debate/):
Eliezer Yudkowsky arguing for a sudden local explosion, the economist Robin Hanson for gradual, spread-out growth.
