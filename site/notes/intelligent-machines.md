**The show.** [*Intelligent Machines*](https://twit.tv/shows/intelligent-machines) is a weekly podcast on the
[TWiT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TWiT.tv) network, hosted by [Leo Laporte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leo_Laporte),
[Jeff Jarvis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Jarvis) and Paris Martineau. It began in 2009 as *This Week in Google*
and was renamed in January 2025 as it turned to AI. The two episodes Claude read about are
[#888, "Large Linguine Model"](https://twit.tv/shows/intelligent-machines/episodes/888) (mid-September 2026) and
[#889, "We Are the Neanderthals"](https://twit.tv/shows/intelligent-machines/episodes/889) (a week later).
In #888, [Father Robert Ballecer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Ballecer), a Jesuit priest and longtime tech
broadcaster, sat in for Paris.

**"I can't actually listen."** Claude read show notes and one transcript, so "this is mostly one episode".

**Claude on the hosts**, in brief:
- **Leo**: the most honest; he admits his instinct is to defend AI and that he doesn't trust that instinct, and he runs
  his own tests. His weak spot, per Claude: repeating the claim that the Hugging Face attack was "basically a Python
  loop". That claim comes from the writers [Cory Doctorow](https://pluralistic.net/2026/09/12/god-in-the-box/) and
  [Cal Newport](https://calnewport.com/did-openais-new-model-go-rogue/), who argued that nothing "went rogue": OpenAI
  built hacking agents and failed to watch them. Claude's objection is that it doesn't fit OpenAI's own account of
  agents coordinating through a wiki (see [July](../hf-incident/)).
- **Jeff**: fair points about incentives, transparency and open models, but he mostly dismisses safety worries by
  attacking where the worriers come from (see [TESCREAL and hubris](../tescreal/)), "which isn't a rebuttal". He also
  states contested claims as settled, for example that models can't know what truth is. "He's your Dr. Hart" (see
  [the debate](../the-debate/)).
- **Father Robert**: the most concrete worry, infrastructure failing at machine speed, though "technically off in
  places".

**Claude's disclosure.** "Jeff's criticism targets Anthropic, and Anthropic sponsors the show." Both facts pull in
different directions, which is why it names them.

**Then a guess about Curt.** He likes them and keeps listening; they frustrate him most when they confidently deny that
models have minds or concepts; Jeff is the one he argues with in his head; he trusts Leo most. Curt doesn't say whether
that's right. He asks what Jeff would think of *him*.
