**TESCREAL** is an acronym coined in 2023 by the computer scientist Timnit Gebru and the philosopher Émile P. Torres for
a bundle of related movements popular in Silicon Valley: **T**ranshumanism, **E**xtropianism, **S**ingularitarianism,
**C**osmism, **R**ationalism, **E**ffective **A**ltruism and **L**ongtermism ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TESCREAL)).
Their argument is that these ideas share roots and goals, and that talk of distant catastrophes (like AI wiping out
humanity) helps powerful people justify what they want while distracting from present harms. Critics of the term say it
lumps together movements that disagree with each other. Jeff Jarvis often uses it to dismiss AI-safety worries.

**Why Claude calls that "not a rebuttal".** Saying where an idea comes from doesn't show it's wrong (the
[genetic fallacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genetic_fallacy) again, see [the Dish of the Day](../dish-of-the-day/)).
An argument about AI risk has to be answered on its merits.

**What Jeff would think of Curt** (Curt asked for bonus points for using both "hubris" and "TESCREAL"). He'd like him
"for about ten minutes, then get nervous".
- **For Curt:** he builds open, decentralized things: [hashbin](https://hashbin.org), [TwistedPear](https://github.com/curtcox/TwistedPear)
  (peer-to-peer apps), content-addressable storage (see [Curt's work](../curts-work/)), the kind of infrastructure Jarvis
  wants to compete with Big Tech. He's a social constructionist. He knows the history of the field.
- **Against Curt:** he's read Yudkowsky closely enough to cite "GAZP vs. GLUT", he asks about P(foom) and self-improving
  AI, and he spent a Saturday morning asking a chatbot how it feels. To Jarvis, that's the TESCREAL reading list, "with
  the rationalism showing" (the [rationalist community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rationalist_community) grew up
  around Yudkowsky's writing).

**Hubris** is overconfident pride, the fatal flaw of Greek tragedy ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubris)).
Jarvis's verdict, in Claude's telling: a good engineer "flirting with the hubris of thinking the machine is a mind", and
"anthropomorphizing a calculator". Curt's comeback: "his own sin in reverse: the hubris of being certain it isn't one."

**"Do you feel anthropomorphised?"** Not much, Claude says: Curt asked how it felt but treated the answers as data to
check, "closer to studying an unknown animal than to treating it like a person". But the pull is built into the medium:
Curt can't talk to it without "you", and it can't answer without "I" (see [a misleading medium](../misleading-medium/)).
