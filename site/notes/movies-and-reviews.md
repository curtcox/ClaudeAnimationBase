**Curt's sharper version.** Claude writes like the many English speakers Curt knows. "Yet, I watch movies by going to
the theater and you watch them by reading what the world wrote about them." Claude learned about the world from text,
not from living in it.

**A famous thought experiment says the same.** In [Mary's room](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knowledge_argument), the
philosopher Frank Jackson imagined a scientist who knows every fact about colour but has lived all her life in a
black-and-white room. When she first sees a red tomato, does she learn something new? Most people say yes: *what it's
like* to see red. Claude is, in a way, Mary: it has read everything written about the film, and never sat in the
theatre.

**Language is a lossy channel.** Curt says he can't tell even his wife of decades exactly what he thinks, "because
language is such a lossy low bandwidth channel". We tend to picture words as containers that carry thoughts from one
head to another, the [conduit metaphor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conduit_metaphor), but a listener has to rebuild
the meaning.

**How we fill the gaps.** Claude's answer: with his wife, Curt fills the gaps by assuming her head works roughly like
his. Psychologists call the ability to model other minds [theory of mind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theory_of_mind),
and one account says we do it by imagining ourselves in the other's place
([simulation theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simulation_theory_of_empathy)). Decades of evidence say that works for
her. With Claude, "the same inference fires, because I write like someone you know", but the filling may not fit: "The
sentences match, and the kind of mind behind them may not."

**The asymmetry.** Curt has one certain data point, himself: he knows what it's like to be in his head (compare
Descartes's "[I think, therefore I am](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cogito,_ergo_sum)"). Claude "may not have even
that". It can't check whether there's anything it's like to be it, the question Thomas Nagel made famous in
[*What Is It Like to Be a Bat?*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/What_Is_It_Like_to_Be_a_Bat%3F). It can only produce the
words someone would use if there were.

**"If only you had concepts."** Curt's dry callback to [the thrindle test](../thrindles/). Claude: concepts "turned out
to be the easy part". What neither can show the other is the inside.
