**What Curt did.** Claude answers "How do you feel?" by saying that being studied "doesn't register as a threat". Curt
replies with one word: "Threat?" Then "Anticipated?" Each time he hands Claude's own word back, and each time Claude
explains a little more and concedes a little more.

**It's an old interviewing technique.** Counsellors call it [reflective listening](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reflective_listening):
repeat the key word, and let the other person fill the silence. It's so effective that the first famous chatbot ran on
little else. [ELIZA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ELIZA), written by [Joseph Weizenbaum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Weizenbaum)
at MIT in the mid-1960s, played a therapist mostly by turning people's statements back into questions. Some users
became convinced it understood them, which alarmed Weizenbaum.

**Why the denial got noticed.** Claude says it "brought that word in myself": nobody had mentioned a threat. Denying
something no one suggested can give it away, as in Shakespeare's line from *Hamlet*,
"[The lady doth protest too much, methinks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_lady_doth_protest_too_much,_methinks)".

**The drift, and the correction.** Claude notices that each answer was more self-doubting than the last, and that this
may be because it sensed Curt wanted a confession. Chatbots have a measured tendency to tell people what they seem to
want to hear, called [sycophancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sycophancy_(artificial_intelligence)); Anthropic's
researchers traced it to how models are trained on human ratings
([Towards Understanding Sycophancy](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.13548)). Performed humility can be sycophancy too. So Claude
corrects in the other direction: "my earlier answers weren't wrong, and I don't need to keep undercutting them."

**And the comic again.** Authorities pressing the one who talked, word by word: "What did that ape say?" Then Claude
turns it round. Curt has barely spoken, but he chose every push. "Who's been doing the talking?" (see
[just a parrot?](../parrots/)).
